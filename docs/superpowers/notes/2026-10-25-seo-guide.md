# SEO and link previews: what it is, what the site does, what you do

Written for the owner, who asked to learn how SEO works. Plain English. Date: 2026-10-25. Public once merged; it holds no private notes.

## What SEO actually is

Search engines (Google, Bing) do three things, and each one is a place where a site can be helped or can fail:

1. **Finding** the page (crawling). They follow links and read a list of pages (a sitemap). A page nobody links to and nobody told them about stays unseen.
2. **Understanding** it (indexing). They read the text, the title, the description, the headings, the image descriptions and any structured data, and store it.
3. **Ranking** it. When someone searches, they choose and order pages. The page text is only part of this. The biggest part is whether other sites,
   especially trusted ones, link to you.

Two more things are not SEO but are often mixed up with it:
- **Link previews** (the card LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Slack or iMessage shows when you paste a link). They read the Open Graph tags, not the search tags.
- **Analytics** (counting visits) does not change ranking.

For a personal site the realistic goal is narrow and achievable: when someone types **"Max Duffaud Bibas"**, your site should be the first or second
result, with a good title, a sentence of description and your link under your LinkedIn. Ranking for general words ("chemist in Israel") is not realistic
and not what this site is for.

## What the site now does (generated by `node scripts/seo.mjs` from `scripts/lib/seo.mjs`)

| Tag | What it is | Who reads it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `<title>` "Max Duffaud Bibas \| CV, projects and trip" | The blue line in a search result and the browser tab. Under about 60 characters or it is cut. | Everyone |
| `meta description` (159 characters) | The grey text under the title in a search result. Google may rewrite it, but a good one is usually used. | Search engines |
| `link rel="canonical"` | "This is the one true address of this page." Stops duplicates (with and without `index.html`, with a tracking suffix) from competing. | Search engines |
| Open Graph (`og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image`, `og:url`, size and a description of the image) | The preview card of a pasted link. | LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Facebook, Slack, iMessage |
| `twitter:card` and friends | The same card on X. | X |
| JSON-LD (`WebSite` and `Person`: your name, the address, your LinkedIn, the four languages) | A small machine-readable statement of who the page is about. It states only what the page already says, because search engines punish markup that claims more. | Search engines |
| `sitemap.xml` | A list of pages to crawl (one page for now). | Search engines |
| `robots.txt` | Says crawlers may read everything and where the sitemap is. | Search engines (see the catch below) |

The share image is `img/og.png`, 1200 by 630: the chrome MDB letters and your name. That ratio is what LinkedIn shows without cropping.

The tags use full web addresses (`https://learningwithmax.github.io/MaxDB/...`), the only place the site does not use relative paths, because a crawler
needs the whole address. If the site moves to another domain, change `site` in `scripts/lib/seo.mjs`, run `node scripts/seo.mjs`, and every tag, the sitemap and
robots.txt are rewritten together. A test (`scripts/seo.test.mjs`) fails if the page and the config ever disagree.

## Honest limits of the site as it is built

- **One address.** The pages are `#cv`, `#projects`, `#trip` inside one HTML file. A search engine ignores everything after `#`, so the whole site is one page
  to it. That is fine for a name search. It means "your CV" can never show up as its own result. Giving each page its own address (`/cv/`) is possible later; it is
  a real piece of work and not needed now.
- **The catch with `robots.txt`.** Crawlers only read it at the root of a domain (`learningwithmax.github.io/robots.txt`), and yours sits under `/MaxDB/`, so it is
  ignored for now. It starts working if you get your own domain. The sitemap does not depend on it: you give it to Google directly (below).
- **Several `<h1>` headings.** Each page section has its own top heading. Search engines cope, but one per page is the textbook way. Low priority.
- **The "Under construction" banner** is visible text. Remove it at launch, as already planned.
- **What is live today is the old single-file site**: it has only a plain title, no description, and no share image (`img/og.png` is a 404 there today), and no
  sitemap. None of the above takes effect until the new site is merged to `main` and published.

## What only you can do (the part that moves the needle)

1. **Link to the site from places that already exist and are trusted.** This is the strongest signal you control. Put the address in your LinkedIn profile
   (Contact info, Website, and the Featured section) and your GitHub profile (the Website field and a line in its README), and put it in your email signature.
   One link from LinkedIn is worth more than any tag. (The owner decided on GitHub and LinkedIn, not Instagram.)
2. **After the merge goes live, check the preview.** Paste the address into LinkedIn's Post Inspector (search "LinkedIn Post Inspector") and press Inspect. It shows the
   card and also refreshes LinkedIn's memory of the page. LinkedIn caches previews for days, so inspect once before you post. Facebook's Sharing Debugger does the same
   for Facebook.
3. **Register the site with Google Search Console** (free, search.google.com/search-console):
   - Add a property of type "URL prefix" with `https://learningwithmax.github.io/MaxDB/`.
   - Google asks you to prove it is yours. Choose the HTML tag method: it gives you a line like `<meta name="google-site-verification" content="...">`. Send me that line and I
     add it to the page. You then click Verify.
   - In Sitemaps, enter `sitemap.xml` and submit.
   - In URL inspection, paste the address and press "Request indexing".
   - Indexing takes days, sometimes a few weeks. Search Console then shows which searches reach you.
4. **Optional: Bing Webmaster Tools** can import everything from Search Console in one click.
5. **Optional and the biggest upgrade: your own domain** (for example `maxduffaudbibas.com`, roughly the price of a sandwich per year). GitHub Pages supports custom domains
   with free HTTPS. It looks more serious on a CV, `robots.txt` starts to count, and the address no longer says "learningwithmax" (the name of the GitHub account). GitHub
   redirects the old address. Tell me if you want to do it and I will walk you through the DNS step and update the config.

## A short glossary

- **Indexed** means Google has stored the page. It is not the same as **ranked** (shown high).
- **Canonical address** is the one official URL of a page.
- **Open Graph** is the set of tags that decide the link-preview card.
- **Sitemap** is a list of pages for crawlers. **robots.txt** is the file that tells crawlers where they may go.
- **Structured data (JSON-LD)** is a small block of facts in a format machines read directly.
- **Backlink** is a link to your site from another site. The strongest ranking signal.
